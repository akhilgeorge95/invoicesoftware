UAE compliance

e-Invoicing, explained properly

The UAE is moving invoicing onto a Peppol-based network. Here is what actually changes, when it applies to your business, and what you need to have in place — without the consultancy jargon.

See the timeline Readiness checklist
What's changing

From PDFs to a structured network

Today most UAE businesses email a PDF and hope it gets paid. Under the mandate, an invoice becomes a structured XML document in the PINT AE format, exchanged over the Peppol network through an Accredited Service Provider (ASP) rather than sent directly.

The UAE model is described as a decentralised five-corner model: you send to your ASP, your ASP validates and routes it over Peppol to your customer's ASP, the customer receives it, and tax data is reported to the Federal Tax Authority.

  • Applies to B2B and B2G transactions
  • Includes self-billing and third-party issuance
  • Scope is broad — VAT registration is not the deciding factor
  • An emailed PDF alone will no longer satisfy the requirement

How it flows

The five-corner model

Nothing about how you write an invoice changes. What changes is the route it takes.

1

You issue

Create the invoice in InvoiceSoftware.ae exactly as you do today.

2

Your ASP validates

We generate PINT AE XML and hand it to your Accredited Service Provider, which checks it against the schema.

3

Peppol delivers

The document is routed over the Peppol network to your customer's service provider.

4

FTA is informed

Tax data is reported to the Federal Tax Authority, and you keep a copy in the archive.

Published timeline

When it applies to you

The rollout is phased by business size. The two dates that matter for any business are the deadline to appoint an ASP and the date invoicing goes live.

Above AED 50 million in revenue? You are in Phase 1 — your ASP needs to be appointed by 30 October 2026, and you go live on 1 January 2027. Everyone else has until 31 March 2027 to appoint, and goes live 1 July 2027.

Get ready

A short readiness checklist

Six things worth doing well before your phase date, most of which take an afternoon.

1 · Confirm your phase

Check your annual revenue against the AED 50 million threshold, and note the two dates that apply to you.

2 · Clean your client data

Structured invoices fail validation on missing details. Make sure every client has a legal name, address and TRN where applicable.

3 · Tidy your item catalogue

Give products and services consistent descriptions, units and tax treatments so each line maps cleanly to the schema.

4 · Choose an ASP

You must appoint an Accredited Service Provider. We can connect to yours, or introduce you to one that fits your size.

5 · Test in the pilot

Voluntary adoption is open. Exchanging a handful of real documents early surfaces data problems while they are still cheap to fix.

6 · Sort out archiving

Decide where issued and received documents are stored, for how long, and who can retrieve them for an audit.
